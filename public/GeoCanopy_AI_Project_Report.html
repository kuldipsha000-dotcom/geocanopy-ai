Section I — Project Overview

Introduction &
Project Background

India's forests are among the most ecologically significant on the planet. They shelter a remarkable share of the world's biodiversity, sustain tens of millions of people, regulate regional weather patterns, and serve as a major terrestrial carbon sink. Yet monitoring these ecosystems at scale — with speed, accuracy, and transparency — has remained a persistent challenge for researchers, policymakers, and the general public alike.

GeoCanopy AI was conceived to close this gap. At its core, the platform is a web-based tool that brings together government-verified forest data, satellite imagery, and artificial intelligence into a single accessible interface. The goal was never to build another dashboard for specialists; it was to create something that a forest officer in Chhattisgarh, a student studying ecology in Kerala, or a journalist covering deforestation in the Sundarbans could actually use and trust.

The platform draws exclusively from published, official sources — the Forest Survey of India (FSI) India State of Forest Reports, MoEFCC CAMPA fund disclosures, and European Space Agency Copernicus satellite telemetry. No projected values or estimates are used in place of recorded data.

2. Problem Statement

Despite India publishing rigorous biennial forest assessments through the FSI, these reports are largely inaccessible to non-specialist audiences. The raw data exists in PDF tables, technical annexures, and dense statistical summaries that demand considerable expertise to interpret. Meanwhile, satellite-derived change data — available freely through programmes like Copernicus — is similarly locked behind technical interfaces that require specialist knowledge to operate.

The result is a significant information gap. Decisions about afforestation, forest diversion, and compensatory planting continue to be made — and scrutinised — largely without the kind of visual, real-time intelligence that modern technology can now provide. GeoCanopy AI was built directly in response to this gap.

"The data on India's forests exists. What has been missing is a bridge between that data and the people who need to understand it — not just forest officials, but citizens, students, journalists, and policymakers at every level."

3. Project Objectives

  1. Present verified FSI forest cover and carbon stock statistics in a visually engaging, publicly accessible format.
  2. Provide interactive satellite imagery comparison tools to make vegetation change perceptible to non-technical audiences.
  3. Integrate an AI-powered assistant capable of answering questions about forest ecology, policy, and regional data with appropriate sourcing.
  4. Track and visualise state-level CAMPA afforestation fund utilisation and plantation targets across major Indian states.
  5. Build the platform as a production-ready web application using open, maintainable technologies — deployable without proprietary infrastructure.

6. Interactive Forest Map

The map module is built on Leaflet.js with ESRI satellite base tiles, rendered over a complete Survey of India–compliant GeoJSON boundary dataset covering all 28 states and 8 union territories. Each state boundary is individually styled to reflect forest canopy density — states with higher canopy percentages display deeper green fills, giving the map immediate visual interpretability without requiring users to read associated data.

Clicking any state triggers a detailed popup displaying forest cover area (sq km), percentage of geographical area, state rank nationally, and the primary FSI classification. The popups are styled with high-contrast dark backgrounds to ensure readability against the satellite map layer beneath. The GeoJSON boundary files are stored as static assets to ensure they are correctly bundled and served without dependency on runtime API calls.

7. Satellite Change Detection Slider

This feature addresses one of the most important — and least accessible — aspects of forest monitoring: temporal change. The module presents three carefully selected sites where notable canopy change has been recorded between 2018 and 2024.

Site 1: Hasdeo Aranya Forest Reserve

Located across Korba and Surguja districts of Chhattisgarh, the Hasdeo complex is one of central India's most ecologically sensitive areas. Sentinel-2 analysis reveals a canopy reduction of approximately 14.8 sq km along the eastern periphery between 2018 and 2024, largely attributed to open-cast mining fringe expansion and associated infrastructure clearing. The AI alert status for this site is classified as HIGH ALERT.

Site 2: Western Ghats Bio-Corridor

The Shivamogga and Uttara Kannada sections of the Western Ghats bio-corridor have shown localised fragmentation in non-protected buffer zones. High-resolution NIR band reflectance comparison indicates a 6.2 sq km canopy reduction, primarily from agricultural encroachment and linear infrastructure expansion. The site remains under active satellite monitoring — MONITORED status.

Site 3: Sundarbans Mangrove Boundary

The South 24 Parganas tidal fringe has experienced a 3.5 sq km mangrove boundary retreat between 2018 and 2024. Sentinel-1 SAR radar telemetry, which can penetrate cloud cover that optical sensors cannot, reveals ongoing tidal erosion and salinity intrusion affecting regeneration cycles. The site is being actively monitored under the MISHTI (Mangrove Initiative for Shoreline Habitats and Tangible Incomes) programme.

8. AI Forest Intelligence Assistant

The AI chat module integrates directly with the Google Gemini 1.5 API to provide natural language responses about Indian forest ecology, FSI data, policy mechanisms, and state-level statistics. The assistant is configured with a detailed system prompt establishing it as a knowledgeable forest monitoring specialist with access to verified FSI data — not a general-purpose chatbot.

Recognising that API connectivity cannot always be guaranteed, the module includes a 36-state offline knowledge engine. This fallback system stores structured data for every Indian state and union territory, allowing the assistant to respond accurately to state-specific queries even without a live API connection. Responses include source attributions wherever quantitative data is presented, maintaining data integrity standards throughout the interaction.

9. CAMPA Afforestation Progress Dashboard

The Compensatory Afforestation Fund Management and Planning Authority (CAMPA) was established under the Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act, 2016, to ensure that forest land diverted for development purposes is compensated through plantation activity on alternative land. The GeoCanopy AI CAMPA dashboard translates fund utilisation and plantation achievement data — drawn from MoEFCC disclosures and the e-GREEN portal — into a searchable, sortable visual interface.

At the national level, the platform reports a total CAMPA fund disbursement of ₹47,436 crore across participating states, with a combined afforestation target of approximately 1.85 million hectares. The national completion rate, as of the most recent available disclosure period, stands at 85.4% — with 1.58 million hectares planted across all monitored states. Eight of the twelve primary states tracked on the platform have received full audit verification through the e-GREEN compliance mechanism.

State-level cards display animated progress bars that fill from zero to the actual completion percentage on scroll, alongside fund release amounts, utilisation rates, and primary plantation species for each state. States are classified under one of three audit statuses: VERIFIED, UNDER AUDIT, or ACTION REQUIRED — each rendered with colour-coded indicators to allow rapid triage.

10. Analytics & Data Visualisation

Six Chart.js visualisations are presented in the analytics section, each targeting a distinct dimension of India's forest data landscape. The charts cover: (1) forest cover trend from 2001 to 2023 as a filled area chart, (2) composition breakdown of very dense, moderately dense, and open forest classifications, (3) year-on-year growth rates, (4) top-10 states by absolute forest area, (5) top-10 states by forest cover as a percentage of geographical area, and (6) net forest cover change between the 2021 and 2023 assessment cycles.

All charts are rendered with a dark-theme colour palette consistent with the overall platform design. The data underlying each chart is sourced from the publicly accessible FSI ISFR reports spanning 2001 to 2023, and the raw values are stored directly in typed TypeScript data files rather than fetched at runtime — ensuring the visualisations remain fully functional without network connectivity.

12. Data Architecture & Sources

A deliberate architectural decision was taken early in development: all quantitative data displayed on the platform should be statically typed, version-controlled, and traceable to a named official source. There are no API calls to third-party data services at runtime. This approach trades real-time data freshness for reliability, integrity, and offline capability — trade-offs that are entirely appropriate given the nature of the data involved (biennial government reports rather than live sensor feeds).

Forest cover statistics are stored in a typed TypeScript module (src/data/forestData.ts) with inline source attribution for every data point. The GeoJSON boundary file for India's states and union territories is served as a static asset from the public/ directory, ensuring it is correctly included in the production build and accessible without authentication.

13. Animation & UI Design System

The platform uses a custom CSS animation library developed specifically for this project, comprising over sixty keyframe animation definitions. The animations are organised into functional categories: scroll-triggered reveal animations (which fire once via IntersectionObserver), continuous ambient animations (floating icons, pulsing live dots, shimmer effects), interaction animations (hover lift, button press, underline grow), and data-entry animations (count-up number transitions, animated progress bar fills).

A key design consideration throughout was respecting the prefers-reduced-motion media query. All continuous and scroll-triggered animations are suppressed for users who have enabled reduced motion in their operating system accessibility settings, ensuring the platform remains fully usable for people with vestibular sensitivities.

15. Limitations & Future Scope

The platform currently reflects FSI data from the 2023 report cycle. Data will require manual updating when the 2025 ISFR is released. The satellite imagery used in the change detection slider uses representative Unsplash imagery rather than live Copernicus tile feeds — integrating direct Sentinel Hub API tiles would significantly increase the scientific credibility of this module but requires a paid subscription and backend CORS proxy.

Planned future additions include real-time visitor count integration via Firebase Realtime Database, direct Sentinel Hub tile integration for the change detection module, user authentication for report saving and export, and mobile-first map optimisation for sub-400px viewports.

16. Conclusion

GeoCanopy AI demonstrates that meaningful environmental data — data that matters for policy, research, and public awareness — can be made genuinely accessible without sacrificing its integrity. By grounding every number in a verifiable official source, and presenting that data through an interface that rewards curiosity rather than demanding expertise, the platform finds a middle path between the technical density of government reports and the oversimplification of popular media coverage.

The platform is production-built, publicly deployable, and entirely open in its data sourcing. It represents a working proof of concept that the intersection of remote sensing, AI, and modern web technology can meaningfully serve India's forest monitoring needs — and that building something both rigorous and usable is not a contradiction in terms.

"Good forest data, presented clearly, is itself a form of accountability. GeoCanopy AI is built on the belief that transparency about India's forests — their extent, their health, their threats — is a public good worth building for."
